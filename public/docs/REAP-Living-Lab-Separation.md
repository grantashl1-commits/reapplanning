# REAP — Living Lab: Separation from Sport Waikato
## Options, Implications and Action Plan
**Classification:** Confidential — For Leanne, Ash, and Board | April 2026

---

## The Question

As REAP grows and the Living Lab develops commercial products, Sport Waikato faces a structural question: should the Living Lab continue as an internal programme, or should it become a separate legal entity?

This document explores that question honestly. It is not advocacy for one outcome — it is a framework for making an informed decision at the right time.

---

## Why Separation Might Be Necessary

**1. Commercial risk ring-fencing.** As REAP generates subscription revenue and potentially significant commercial activity, Sport Waikato's charitable status may be tested. Revenue from commercial products must demonstrably serve charitable purposes. A subsidiary company structure can ring-fence commercial operations and protect the parent trust.

**2. IP ownership clarity.** Who owns REAP? Currently, it is a programme of Sport Waikato. If Leanne and Ash develop products within their employment, those products belong to Sport Waikato. A separate entity creates a defined IP ownership structure.

**3. Commercial freedom.** A charitable trust has restrictions on what commercial activities it can pursue. A separate company has none (subject to its own constitution). As Living Lab products expand into licensing, international markets, and commercial data partnerships, a company structure provides operational freedom a charitable trust does not.

**4. Career and equity interests of Leanne and Ash.** If they are creating commercially valuable products, a separate entity in which they hold equity aligns their financial interests with the success of the products they build.

**5. Funder and investor access.** A separate company can take investment (equity, grants from private foundations, impact investment) that a charitable trust cannot. If the Living Lab is to scale beyond what Sport Waikato's operational budget allows, external capital becomes relevant.

## Structural Options

### Option A: Sport Waikato Subsidiary Company (Recommended for Year 1–2)

**Structure:** Sport Waikato creates a wholly owned subsidiary company (NZ Limited company). Living Lab products, including REAP, are owned by the subsidiary. Leanne and Ash are employed by Sport Waikato but appointed as directors of the subsidiary.

**Revenue flow:** Subsidiary generates subscription revenue and commercial income. Subsidiary pays a management fee or licence fee to Sport Waikato. Remaining profit is distributed to Sport Waikato (the sole shareholder) as dividends/distributions.

**IP ownership:** IP held by the subsidiary. Sport Waikato owns the subsidiary. Effective IP control remains with Sport Waikato.

**Leanne and Ash:** Remain Sport Waikato employees. Hold director positions in the subsidiary. No equity.

**Pros:**
- Clean separation of commercial risk
- Sport Waikato retains full control and IP ownership
- No conflict of interest issues for Leanne and Ash
- Simpler to set up than a full spin-out
- Revenue still flows to the charitable trust

**Cons:**
- Leanne and Ash have no equity upside — limits entrepreneurial motivation
- Still subject to Sport Waikato board oversight
- Commercial decisions still require trust board approval at significant thresholds

**Timeline:** Could be established before Season 1. Recommended if Living Lab is generating >$50k/year.

---

### Option B: Independent Company — Leanne and Ash as Owners (Recommended for Year 3+)

**Structure:** Leanne and Ash resign from Sport Waikato and incorporate Living Lab NZ Limited as an independent company. They are shareholders (e.g., 50/50 or according to contribution) and directors. Living Lab contracts services to Sport Waikato (research delivery, programme management) and to other clients.

**REAP ownership:** Either:
- (a) REAP IP transferred to Living Lab NZ Ltd under a commercial agreement (Sport Waikato receives a royalty or licence fee)
- (b) REAP IP remains with Sport Waikato; Living Lab NZ Ltd operates REAP under exclusive licence

**Revenue flow:** Living Lab NZ Ltd generates subscription revenue. Contracts back research and programme delivery to Sport Waikato. Sport Waikato remains a key client.

**Conflict of interest:** At this point there is no conflict — Leanne and Ash are not Sport Waikato employees. The relationship is client/contractor.

**Pros:**
- Full commercial freedom
- Leanne and Ash have equity upside — strong entrepreneurial alignment
- Can take external investment
- Can pursue international and commercial opportunities without trust board approval
- Clear entrepreneurial identity

**Cons:**
- Leanne and Ash lose employment security (salary, entitlements)
- IP negotiation with Sport Waikato can be complex and contentious
- Sport Waikato loses in-house Living Lab capability
- Requires careful legal documentation of the Sport Waikato relationship to avoid conflicts

**Key question:** Can they continue as Sport Waikato employees AND own Living Lab NZ Ltd? Technically yes, with board disclosure and approval — but it creates ongoing conflict of interest management challenges. The cleaner path is formal separation.

---

### Option C: Living Lab as a Separate Charitable Trust

**Structure:** Living Lab is incorporated as a separate charitable trust under the Charitable Trusts Act 1957. Governed by its own board of trustees (may include Sport Waikato representation). Leanne and Ash serve as trustees or employees of the new trust.

**Pros:**
- Access to philanthropic and government grant funding independently
- Charitable status creates credibility with health sector and research partners
- Research mandate is formally protected

**Cons:**
- No equity for Leanne and Ash (charitable trusts cannot distribute to individuals)
- Commercial activity still constrained by charitable purpose
- More complex governance structure
- Leanne and Ash as trustees cannot benefit financially from the trust

**Assessment:** Good structure for the research function. Not appropriate as the primary commercial vehicle.

---

### Option D: Continue as Internal Programme (Current State)

**Assessment:** Viable for Year 1 and early Year 2. Becomes increasingly difficult to manage as commercial activity grows. Revisit at the end of Season 1.

## The Employment Question: Can Leanne and Ash Stay at Sport Waikato and Own Living Lab?

This is the most sensitive question in the document and requires specific legal advice. The general position under NZ law:

- **It is legally possible** for an employee to own and operate a separate business, with employer knowledge and consent
- **It requires full disclosure** to Sport Waikato's Board and formal written approval
- **Conflict of interest management** is essential: any decision Leanne or Ash make in their Sport Waikato capacity that affects Living Lab (a company they own) must be declared and managed (e.g., abstaining from relevant board votes)
- **Employment agreements** must be reviewed — many NZ employment agreements include IP assignment clauses that mean work done during employment belongs to the employer
- **Fair Trading Act:** Living Lab's commercial activities must not create confusion with Sport Waikato's programmes

**Practical reality:** If they remain employed by Sport Waikato while owning Living Lab, every commercial decision they make will require conflict management. This becomes operationally burdensome. The cleaner path is formal separation when the time is right.

## IP Transfer Considerations

When Living Lab becomes a separate entity, the IP question must be resolved before any transition:

| IP item | Current owner | Transfer option |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| REAP brand and trade marks | Sport Waikato | Transfer at agreed value, or licence |
| App code and technology | Sport Waikato (via employment) | Transfer at agreed value, or licence |
| Research data and publications | Sport Waikato | Research licence to Living Lab |
| Domain names | Sport Waikato | Transfer or licence |
| Living Lab brand | Sport Waikato | Transfer or licence |

A formal IP valuation and transfer agreement is required. This takes time and legal resource. Plan for 3–6 months of transition work.
