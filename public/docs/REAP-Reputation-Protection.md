# REAP — Reputation Protection Plan
**Classification:** Internal Strategy | Sport Waikato Living Lab | April 2026

---

## Foundation Position

Sport Waikato's decision to build REAP is a bold one. Bold decisions attract bold responses. The board should be fully prepared for the reality that some media coverage of REAP will be negative or sceptical, particularly in the launch period.

This is not a reason to be less bold. It is a reason to have a clear, confident plan for responding to adversity before it happens.

The organisation that designed "THE KILLER MOVEMENT APP" has already signalled it is not playing it safe. The response to negative coverage must match that signal: confident, evidence-based, and mission-led — not defensive, apologetic, or reactive.

---

## Scenario 1: DIA Gambling Challenge

**Trigger:** The Department of Internal Affairs formally contacts Sport Waikato to indicate REAP may constitute unlicensed gambling under the Gambling Act 2003.

**Immediate response (within 4 hours):**
- Cease all prize draw activity immediately — no draws pending or planned
- Contact Shelley (legal counsel) immediately — this is her call to lead
- Do not make any public statement until legal advice is received
- Notify board same day

**Longer-term response:**
- If DIA is satisfied by the performance-award structure: document their position, continue
- If DIA requires structural change: modify the draw mechanism under legal guidance, re-launch
- If DIA requires a licence: assess whether obtaining a licence is feasible under Sport Waikato's charitable trust structure; if not, remove prizes from the product

**Public narrative (if it becomes public):**
*"Sport Waikato took proactive steps to seek informal DIA guidance before launching REAP. We are working constructively with the DIA to confirm our prize structure is compliant. REAP's performance awards are skill-based — they go to whoever has moved the most — not to randomly selected participants. We are confident in our structure and are happy to work through any questions the DIA has."*

**What never to say:** Anything that implies the product was designed to skirt gambling law. It was designed to comply. Say so.

## Scenario 2: Participant Welfare Incident

**Trigger:** A participant is hospitalised, experiences a mental health crisis, or is otherwise seriously harmed in a way that is plausibly connected to REAP participation or an elimination notification.

**Immediate response (within 2 hours):**
- Human welfare is the absolute first priority. All other considerations are secondary.
- Follow the Product Harm Protocol (see REAP-Product-Harm-Protocol.md) — Level 3 crisis response
- CEO is notified immediately
- Board is notified same day
- Legal advice engaged immediately
- Do not make any public statement without legal advice

**If the incident becomes public:**
- Do not be defensive. Do not immediately cite the Terms of Participation.
- Express genuine concern for the individual
- Confirm that a welfare protocol exists and was followed
- Do not disclose personal details of the individual under any circumstances

**Prepared statement (adapt to circumstances):**
*"Sport Waikato takes participant wellbeing extremely seriously. We are aware of a situation involving a REAP participant and we are in contact with them directly. REAP includes explicit wellbeing screening, crisis resource signposting in all elimination communications, and a documented welfare protocol that we follow for any participant in distress. We are reviewing this situation carefully."*

**What never to say:** "The Terms of Participation are clear that Sport Waikato is not responsible..." — in a welfare incident, leading with legal disclaimers is reputationally catastrophic and ethically wrong.

**Follow-up actions:**
- Review and update the welfare protocol based on learnings
- Consider proactive communication to all participants about support resources
- Explore formal mental health partnership acceleration

## Scenario 3: Celebrity Controversy

**Trigger:** A celebrity ambassador becomes the subject of public controversy (scandal, allegations, controversial statements) during or after the October soft launch.

**Immediate response (within 24 hours):**
- Do not publicly comment on the celebrity's controversy unless directly asked
- Quietly cease resharing the celebrity's REAP content on Sport Waikato channels
- Review the Ambassador Agreement termination clause
- If the celebrity's controversy is directly contrary to Sport Waikato's values (e.g., health messaging contradiction, serious misconduct), formally terminate the agreement

**If media asks about the association:**
*"Sport Waikato is focused on REAP's launch and the health mission behind it. We're not going to comment on [celebrity's] personal situation."*

**Prevention:** The ambassador brief and agreement explicitly includes a controversy clause. Use it. Tier 1 ambassadors (see Celebrity Ambassador Agreement) have been selected for low controversy risk profiles.

## Scenario 4: Data Breach

**Trigger:** An unauthorised party accesses participant personal data, including health-related activity data.

**Immediate response (within 2 hours):**
- Contain: revoke compromised credentials, disable affected access points
- Assess: what data was accessed, how many participants affected, what type of data
- Notify: if the breach is likely to cause serious harm — Privacy Commissioner must be notified; affected individuals notified as soon as practicable
- Legal advice engaged immediately
- CEO and board notified same day

**Public narrative (if it becomes public):**
*"Sport Waikato became aware of a data security incident affecting [number] REAP participants. We immediately contained the breach and are contacting all affected participants directly. We have notified the Privacy Commissioner as required. The security of participant data is a priority and we are taking immediate steps to prevent recurrence."*

**Prevention:** The security checklist in the TO DO list (REAP-TO-DO-List.md) includes all the technical measures to reduce this risk. These must be implemented before launch.

## Scenario 5: Sustained Negative Media Campaign

**Trigger:** A journalist or media outlet publishes an adversarial story about REAP — framing it as gambling, irresponsible, harmful to vulnerable people, or inappropriate for a charitable trust.

**The right tone:** Confident. Evidence-led. Mission-forward. Not defensive.

**Who responds:** CEO (Leanne) only. All media enquiries go to the CEO. The Living Lab Lead does not speak to media without CEO approval.

**The REAP media brief** (see REAP-Media-Handling.md) contains prepared answers to every anticipated hard question. These must be rehearsed before the launch.

**What to do before the story publishes:**
- If given advance notice of the story, request to review for factual inaccuracies (standard practice, does not require cooperation with the angle)
- Provide the journalist with the behavioural science evidence, the legal position (summary only — not full legal documents), and the charitable trust mandate context
- If the journalist is genuinely trying to understand the product, answer their questions directly and confidently
- If the journalist is pursuing a predetermined angle, answer on the record for what goes in the paper, and let the evidence speak

**If the story publishes:**
- Do not engage in social media wars
- Publish a clear, factual response on REAP's own channels if the story contains factual errors
- Contact the editor (not the journalist) if there are material factual inaccuracies — editors correct errors
- Use the negative coverage as an opportunity to state the positive mission clearly

**The bold response model:**
*"We welcome scrutiny of REAP. We built something deliberately different and we're confident in why. If anyone wants to understand the behavioural science, the legal framework, or why a publicly-funded charitable trust is running a survival game, we're happy to explain."*

## Scenario 6: Competitor or Imitation Product Launches

**Trigger:** A well-resourced competitor announces or launches a similar daily-movement elimination product in New Zealand.

**Response:**
- Do not comment on the competitor publicly (looks insecure)
- Accelerate community-building activities: announce the next season, the RST expansion, the Friday 13th event, the Annual Championship
- Privately assess whether the competitor is a licensing opportunity or a genuine competitor
- Remind stakeholders of REAP's provenance: "Sport Waikato built this first. We have the data, the community, and the Living Lab behind us."

See REAP-Competitive-Strategy.md for the full response framework.

## Proactive Reputation Protection: Things to Do Before Any Crisis

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Funder briefing (Sport NZ) before public launch | No funder should be surprised by media coverage |
| Board briefing with full risk documentation | Board comfort and prepared responses |
| Mental health partnership announced at launch | Addresses the largest reputational risk proactively |
| DIA informal enquiry lodged before launch | Demonstrates responsible operator behaviour |
| Legal sign-off published as a fact (not details) | "Our Terms and prize structure have received formal legal sign-off" is a powerful statement |
| Welfare protocol document on file | If a welfare incident occurs, "we had a documented protocol" is the most important thing to be able to say |
| Celebrity briefing with clear expectations | Reduces ambassador-generated reputation risk |
| Staff briefed and aligned | Staff fielding public queries should not be surprised or ill-equipped |

## The Underlying Thread

The organisations that protect their reputation best are not the ones who never take risks. They are the ones who take calculated risks with their eyes open, who document what they considered and why, and who respond to adversity with confidence rather than retreat.

Sport Waikato has gone big. It has gone bold. It has gone different. The reputation protection plan is not an apology for that choice. It is the infrastructure that makes it sustainable.
