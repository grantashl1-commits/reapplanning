### SECTION: WHY IT WORKS

**Heading:** The Psychology of Survival

**Subheading:** This isn't motivation. This is architecture.

**Block 1 — Loss Aversion**
Humans are wired to avoid loss more powerfully than they pursue gain. REAP doesn't reward movement — it threatens elimination for inactivity. That asymmetry is intentional. Research consistently shows loss-framed incentives drive stronger behavioural compliance than reward-framed ones.

**Block 2 — Daily Accountability**
Streaks work. Daily rituals stick. REAP gives you one clear non-negotiable action each day. No decision fatigue. No "what should I do today?" Just: did you move? Yes or no.

**Block 3 — Structured Simplicity**
21 minutes. Zone 2. Midnight cutoff. The rules are simple enough that your brain can't negotiate with them. The deadline is the game mechanic. The game mechanic is the habit.

**Block 4 — Social Pressure**
The Survival Board is public. Your friends can see who's still alive. That visibility creates low-grade social accountability — the same reason people finish group fitness challenges they'd quit alone.

**Block 5 — Sport Waikato Living Lab**
REAP is developed and operated by the Sport Waikato Living Lab — a behavioural health research initiative that tests novel physical activity interventions with real populations. This isn't a startup with a theory. It's a charitable trust with a mission.

### SECTION: WHAT REAP IS

**Heading:** A Game With Consequences

**Copy:**
Most fitness apps reward you for moving. Streaks, badges, leaderboards — dopamine dressed up as progress.

REAP inverts that model entirely.

In REAP, movement is not optional. It's survival. You move 21 minutes of Zone 2 cardio every day, or you are eliminated at midnight. There is no snooze. There is no grace period. There is the Reap, and there is survival — and the only variable is whether you moved today.

This is not about aesthetics. It is not about weight loss. It is not about optimisation.

It is about inactivity — one of the world's leading preventable causes of early death — and the uncomfortable truth that knowing that fact has never, on its own, been enough to make people move.

REAP is what happens when you stop trying to make movement aspirational and start making inactivity cost something.

---

### SECTION: THE SCIENCE

**Heading:** Why 21 Minutes. Why Every Day.

**Copy:**
The World Health Organisation recommends adults accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week. That works out to 21–43 minutes per day.

REAP uses the lower bound — 21 minutes — as its daily minimum. Not because it's easy. Because it's achievable by almost anyone with functional mobility, and because the evidence is unambiguous: meeting this threshold consistently, over time, is associated with significantly reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, depression, and all-cause mortality.

The problem is not that people don't know this. The problem is that knowing it doesn't change behaviour.

**Subheading:** Inactivity Is the Baseline

**Copy:**
Research on behavioural change consistently shows that human beings default to inactivity — not because they're lazy, but because modern environments have removed the natural movement demands that governed human physiology for millennia. We are the first species in history whose primary challenge is getting ourselves to move voluntarily.

REAP works with this reality rather than against it. Instead of adding a positive incentive on top of inactivity (a badge, a streak count), it attaches a negative consequence to inactivity — elimination from a game you're already paying to play.

Loss aversion is one of the most robust findings in behavioural economics. People work harder to avoid losing something than to gain something of equivalent value. REAP applies that directly to daily movement.

**Subheading:** Zone 2 — Why We Chose It

**Copy:**
Zone 2 cardio — moderate intensity, conversational pace, sustained effort — is the most accessible and most evidenced category of physical activity for long-term health outcomes. It requires no equipment, no gym membership, no technical skill. Walking at a brisk pace qualifies. It is appropriate for the overwhelming majority of adults.

It is also the zone most frequently sacrificed when life gets busy, because it doesn't feel urgent. REAP makes it urgent — every single day.

### SECTION: ZONE 2 EXPLAINED

**Heading:** What Is Zone 2?

**Copy:**
Zone 2 refers to moderate-intensity cardiovascular exercise. Technically, it corresponds to approximately 60–70% of your maximum heart rate — but in practical terms, the simplest test is the talk test:

**Zone 2 = You can hold a conversation, but you couldn't comfortably sing.**

If you're gasping, you've gone too hard. If you could recite a speech without effort, you haven't gone hard enough. The sweet spot is sustained, moderate effort.

**Activities that count:**
- Brisk walking (outdoors or treadmill)
- Light jogging
- Cycling (road, trail, or stationary)
- Swimming laps
- Rowing (moderate pace)
- Elliptical
- Dancing (sustained moderate effort)
- Hiking

**Activities that do not count:**
- Resistance/strength training alone
- Yoga or stretching
- Very light walking (strolling)
- High-intensity intervals (these exceed Zone 2)
- Recreational sport with significant rest periods

**A note on wearables:**
Most modern wearable devices (Garmin, Apple Watch, Fitbit, Polar, Whoop, and others) record Zone 2 data automatically. REAP uses your device's heart rate zone data to verify compliance. If your device doesn't record heart rate zones, time-based verification using pace/speed thresholds applies. Full device compatibility details below.

---

### SECTION: THE MIDNIGHT REAP

**Heading:** What Happens at Midnight

**Copy:**
At midnight in your local time zone, REAP checks your activity data.

If you have completed 21+ minutes of verified Zone 2 activity, you survive. The counter ticks over. A new day begins.

If you have not, your survival status is updated to "Eliminated." You will receive a notification. Your place on the Survival Board changes. Your eligibility for subsequent spot prize draws ends.

There is no appeal. There is no technical grace period for syncing issues (you have until 11:59pm — sync before midnight, not after). There is no manual override.

**Why the hard cutoff?**
Because a soft cutoff isn't a cutoff. The midnight deadline is the game. If there are exceptions, the consequence disappears — and the consequence is what drives the behaviour. We know this is strict. That's the point.

---

### SECTION: REDEMPTION DAYS

**Heading:** Redemption Days — Your One Lifeline

**Copy:**
Each participant receives **two Redemption Days** per season.

A Redemption Day is a pre-declared rest day. You apply for it through the REAP app before midnight. On a Redemption Day, you are not eliminated for missing your 21-minute requirement — but your participation and spot prize eligibility continues.

**Rules:**
- You must declare your Redemption Day before midnight on the day you wish to use it (not retroactively)
- Redemption Days cannot be carried over or transferred
- Using both Redemption Days does not affect your spot prize eligibility — you are still considered an active survivor
- Redemption Days are intended for illness, injury, or genuine unavoidable circumstances — but no documentation is required

**Why only two?**
Because the habit is the product. Two rest days across 30 days is generous relative to the WHO's own recommendation (which allows for rest). More than two, and the daily commitment ceases to function as a commitment.

### SECTION: FULL FAQ

**Heading:** Questions. Answered Without Warmth.

**Q: Can I split my 21 minutes across multiple activities?**
A: Yes. Two 10.5-minute Zone 2 sessions on the same day count toward your daily total.

**Q: Does my device have to sync automatically?**
A: Automatic syncing is strongly recommended. Manual syncing is permitted but must be completed before midnight. Post-midnight sync attempts do not count toward the previous day.

**Q: What if my device fails or loses data?**
A: Documented device failure may be considered under the disputes process (see Rules). Accidental non-sync is not a valid dispute ground. Always sync before 11pm — don't leave it to midnight.

**Q: Can I join after the season has started?**
A: No. All participants must join before Day 1 of each season. Mid-season entry is not available.

**Q: What if I'm injured?**
A: Use a Redemption Day. If your injury spans more than two days, contact REAP Support. Extended medical exemptions are assessed case by case and are not guaranteed. REAP is not designed for participants currently unable to perform any moderate physical activity.

**Q: Is REAP safe if I have a heart condition?**
A: REAP involves daily moderate-intensity cardiovascular activity. Before starting, consult your GP or specialist if you have a cardiovascular condition, recent surgery, or any medical reason to limit exercise. By joining REAP, you confirm you are medically able to participate. See our Terms of Participation.

**Q: What age do I need to be to join?**
A: You must be 18 or older to participate. No exceptions.

**Q: Can I participate if I'm pregnant?**
A: REAP's daily movement requirement is compatible with many stages of healthy pregnancy — but you should seek GP clearance before participating and throughout the season. Moderate exercise during healthy pregnancy is generally supported by clinical evidence, but REAP cannot provide individual medical guidance.

**Q: What happens to my data when I'm eliminated?**
A: Your account remains active. Your historical activity data is retained and accessible to you. You can re-enroll in the following season. See our Privacy Policy for full data handling details.

**Q: Can I pause my subscription?**
A: Subscriptions cannot be paused mid-season. You may cancel, but season fees are non-refundable once the season has begun. See Terms of Participation.

### SECTION: SPOKESPERSON Q&A

**Heading:** Pre-Prepared Q&A for Journalists

**Q: Why does a charitable trust run a survival game?**
A: Because charitable trusts exist to serve their community, and the Waikato community — like every community globally — has an inactivity problem that polite wellness interventions haven't solved. Sport Waikato's Living Lab exists specifically to test whether unconventional approaches work. REAP is an unconventional approach. The survival frame is the mechanism, not the message. The message is: move every day.

**Q: Why is there a prize? Does that make this gambling?**
A: No. REAP is not a gambling product. The prize is a minor, incidental feature funded by Sport Waikato outside of subscription revenue. Participation in REAP requires payment — but the payment is for access to the game, not for a chance at a prize. Prize eligibility is an automatic consequence of surviving, not the reason to participate. The Gambling Act 2003 has been considered in the product's design and the prize structure is designed to fall outside its scope. REAP's legal framework has been reviewed by external counsel.

**Q: What's the dark aesthetic about? Is this appropriate for a health organisation?**
A: The aesthetic is deliberate. "Cute death" — the brand's own description — is a creative choice to reflect the reality that inactivity is a serious public health problem, without the earnestness that causes most people to disengage with health messaging. The WHO data is not cheerful. Pretending otherwise is the wellness industry's problem, not ours. The tone is playful about the game while being entirely serious about the movement requirement.

**Q: Is this safe for people with mental health issues? Could the elimination mechanic cause distress?**
A: Elimination from a game you've chosen to play is a designed consequence, not a hostile act. REAP is transparent about its mechanics before any money changes hands. That said, Sport Waikato takes this question seriously. The product includes clear health screening in the sign-up flow. The game is not recommended for people in acute mental health crisis. REAP's communication around elimination is factual and non-shaming — you're out, not a failure. This is an area where we continue to gather participant feedback and remain willing to adapt.

**Q: Isn't $14.99 exclusionary?**
A: It is a real cost. Sport Waikato is aware that not everyone can access a subscription product, which is why the corporate and sponsored group model exists — and why REAP revenue funds community programmes for people who can't pay. REAP is not designed to be the only solution. It is designed to work for the people it can reach, and to fund solutions for the people it can't.

**Copy:**
The Living Lab is Sport Waikato's research and innovation initiative. Its purpose is simple and slightly uncomfortable: to test whether what we think works in physical activity promotion actually works, in real conditions, with real people, and to build new interventions when the evidence says we need a different approach.

The Living Lab operates on three principles:

**1. Scepticism by default.**
We don't assume a new idea will work. We design for failure, measure honestly, and report what we find — including when the answer is "this didn't work."

**2. Real populations, not controlled samples.**
Our interventions are tested on actual community members — adults with real lives, inconsistent habits, and no particular motivation to participate in research. If it works here, it probably works.

**3. Commercial models that fund community outcomes.**
Where a Living Lab product generates revenue, that revenue returns to Sport Waikato's community programmes. REAP is designed to generate subscription revenue that funds the programmes that reach people who'd never pay $14.99 for an app.
