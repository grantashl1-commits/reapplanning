Sport Waikato is the Regional Sports Trust for the Waikato region. It operates under a Sport New Zealand funding arrangement and holds charitable registration. Its Living Lab is a research and innovation unit that develops community health and physical activity products, including digital fitness platforms.

### 2. Product Description: Survive the REAP

"Survive the REAP" (hereinafter "REAP") is a 30-day digital fitness survival game delivered via a mobile/web application. The product is designed to increase sustained moderate-intensity physical activity among New Zealand adults and will launch in November 2026.

#### 2.1 Subscription and Entry

Participants subscribe to REAP for $13.00 NZD per calendar month (the "subscription fee"). The subscription provides access to:

- The REAP fitness tracking platform and mobile application;
- A 30-day structured fitness programme;
- A community and accountability environment;
- Access to performance data and progress tracking;
- Eligibility to participate in the season's prize draws (as described below).

The subscription is a standard commercial arrangement for access to a fitness service. It is not framed, marketed, or structured as payment for a chance to win a prize. No participant pays a separate fee or additional amount for entry into any draw.

#### 2.2 The Survival Mechanic

Each season runs for 30 consecutive days. To remain an "Active Survivor" and avoid elimination, a participant must:

- Achieve a continuous minimum of **21 minutes** of physical activity at **Zone 2 heart rate intensity** (approximately 60–70% of maximum heart rate) each calendar day; and
- Log that activity through a connected wearable device or manual entry verified against the platform's criteria, before **midnight New Zealand Time (NZT)** on that calendar day.

A participant who fails to meet this daily target on any given day is **eliminated** from that season. Elimination is automatic and irrevocable within a season. An eliminated participant loses eligibility for all remaining prize draws in that season but retains access to the platform for the remainder of their subscription period.

The survival mechanic is entirely **skill-dependent**: a participant's continued eligibility is determined solely by their own daily physical effort, consistency, and fitness. There is no element of luck, chance, or random selection involved in whether a participant remains an Active Survivor.

#### 2.3 Prize Draw Structure

REAP conducts **seven prize draws per season**, on Days 7, 13, 14, 21, 24, 28, and 30. Each draw awards a prize of **$666.00 NZD**. The total prize commitment per season is **$4,662.00 NZD**.

Each draw operates as follows:

1. At the time of the draw (midnight NZT on the designated draw day), the platform identifies all participants who are **Active Survivors** at that moment — that is, all participants who have successfully met the daily Zone 2 target every day up to and including that draw day.
2. One winner is selected at random from the pool of Active Survivors at that time.
3. The prize of $666.00 NZD is paid to the winner.

**Critical features of the draw pool:**

- Only Active Survivors are eligible. A participant who has been eliminated at any point prior to the draw day is **excluded** from that draw pool regardless of their initial subscription.
- The eligible pool therefore **contracts** over the course of the season as participants are eliminated through failure to meet the daily skill-based target.
- At later draw days (e.g., Days 28 and 30), the eligible pool may be very small — potentially fewer than ten participants — because only those demonstrating sustained high-level physical fitness performance remain.
- A participant cannot enter the draw pool for Day 21 without having performed 21 consecutive days of qualifying Zone 2 exercise.

#### 2.4 Prize Pool Funding

The prize pool ($4,662 per season) is funded entirely from subscription revenue collected from participants in that season. There is no separately charged "entry fee" for the draws. No participant pays anything beyond the $13 monthly subscription — which is charged for access to the fitness platform, not for the chance to win a prize.

### 3. The Legal Question

Sport Waikato seeks the Department's written view on the following specific question:

> **Does the REAP prize draw structure — being a random draw conducted among Active Survivors only (participants who have individually earned eligibility through daily skill-based physical performance), funded from subscription revenue, and incidental to a commercial fitness subscription service — constitute "gambling", a "lottery", a "game of chance", or a "promotional competition" under the Gambling Act 2003? If it constitutes gambling, what class of gambling does it represent?**

---

### 4. Sport Waikato's Position: REAP is Not Gambling

Sport Waikato submits that REAP's prize draw structure does not constitute gambling within the meaning of the Gambling Act 2003 for the following reasons.

#### 4.1 The Statutory Definition of Gambling: Section 4, Gambling Act 2003

Section 4(1) of the Gambling Act 2003 defines "gambling" as an activity in which:

> (a) participants pay, or agree to pay, money or money's worth to take part; and
> (b) participants have a chance to win money or money's worth; and
> (c) the result is determined wholly or partly by chance.

All three limbs must be satisfied. Sport Waikato submits that REAP fails at least two of these three limbs, and arguably all three.

**Limb (a) — Payment to take part in the prize draw:**

Participants pay the $13 subscription fee for access to the fitness platform, not "to take part" in a prize draw. The subscription confers substantial commercial value entirely independent of any prize: the platform, programme, data, and community. The draw is incidental to — not the purpose of — the subscription. There is no separate or additional charge for entry into the draw. The DIA's published guidance distinguishes between payment for a commercial product or service and payment for a chance to win, noting that the former does not satisfy limb (a).

**Limb (b) — Chance to win:**

A participant's eligibility to be included in the draw pool is not a matter of chance. It is entirely determined by their own skill, fitness, and daily effort. A participant who does not perform 21 continuous minutes of Zone 2 cardio every day up to a given draw day has no chance to win at that draw — not because of random exclusion, but because they have been skill-eliminated. The "chance to win" in the conventional gambling sense (where all entrants have an equal probabilistic chance) does not exist in REAP. Only those who have earned eligibility through sustained physical performance are in the pool.

**Limb (c) — Result determined wholly or partly by chance:**

Even if limbs (a) and (b) were satisfied, the ultimate outcome in REAP is determined partly by skill (the performance requirement) and partly by chance (the random selection within the eligible pool). The New Zealand courts and DIA guidance have recognised that activities with a substantial skill component do not satisfy this limb in the same manner as pure games of chance. We note that the degree to which skill determines outcome in REAP is substantial — participation in the final draws requires 28–30 consecutive days of qualifying daily exercise, a significant and non-trivial physical achievement.

#### 4.2 Lottery Analysis: Section 4, Gambling Act 2003 and Lotteries Act Principles

"Lottery" is defined within the Gambling Act 2003 by reference to the three-limb gambling definition above with an emphasis on schemes where consideration is paid for a ticket or entry that carries a chance of winning determined wholly by chance. REAP does not issue "tickets" or "entries" to a draw. Eligibility in the draw pool is earned through performance — it is not purchased. The draw pool is therefore not equivalent to a pool of ticket-holders. The structure is materially different from a lottery.

#### 4.3 Game of Chance Analysis

A "game of chance" under s4 requires that the result be determined wholly or partly by chance. As noted above, the threshold question in REAP is not determined by chance at all — it is determined by skill (daily Zone 2 compliance). The random selection within the eligible pool is analogous to a random draw among qualifying employees for a staff award, or a random draw among competition finalists who have all completed a qualifying round: the random element is subordinate to a primary skill-based qualification.

#### 4.4 Promotional Competition Carve-Out

To the extent any doubt remains, Sport Waikato submits that REAP falls within the promotional competition framework recognised under New Zealand law. A promotional competition — being a competition that is ancillary to a genuine commercial product or service, where entrants compete (or qualify) through skill or effort and the prize draw is the mechanism for selecting among qualifying entrants — is generally not classified as gambling. REAP's draws are ancillary to a genuine commercial fitness subscription product. The subscription would have full commercial value even if no prize draws existed.

#### 4.5 Operator Intent

Sport Waikato's intent in offering the prize draws is to provide a motivational incentive for physical activity participation — a public health objective. It is not to operate a gambling or lottery product. The prize pool is modest relative to subscription revenue, deliberately so, to ensure the draws remain incidental to the fitness product. Marketing materials emphasise the fitness challenge, the community, and the survival mechanic — not the prize draws.

### 1. Purpose

This memorandum accompanies the informal enquiry letter submitted to the Department of Internal Affairs' Gambling Compliance Group on 7 April 2026. ### 2. Legislative Framework

The primary statute is the **Gambling Act 2003** (the Act). The Act regulates gambling in New Zealand, establishes the four classes of gambling, and vests regulatory authority in the Secretary for Internal Affairs.

### 3. Statutory Analysis: The Three-Limb Test

#### 3.1 Limb (a): Payment to Take Part

Section 4(1)(a) requires that participants "pay, or agree to pay, money or money's worth to take part" in the activity.

The phrase "to take part" has been interpreted by the DIA as referring to payment whose purpose is participation in the chance-to-win activity itself. Where payment is made for a distinct commercial product or service, and participation in a prize draw is incidental to that commercial transaction, limb (a) is not satisfied.

**Application to REAP:**

The $13 subscription fee is charged for access to a fitness platform delivering a structured 30-day programme, wearable integration, community features, performance tracking, and expert content. These benefits have substantial and independent commercial value. The subscription would be commercially viable and legally unproblematic in the complete absence of any prize draw.

The prize draw is described in terms of service as an "incidental motivational incentive" and is not the primary commercial offering. It is not promoted as the reason to subscribe. There is no evidence that the subscription fee has been structured, inflated, or calibrated in any way to reflect the cost of participating in a draw.

Accordingly, limb (a) is not satisfied. The payment is for a fitness service, not for a chance to win.

**Risk note:** If REAP were ever to market the draws as a primary subscription benefit (e.g., "Subscribe for your chance at $666"), this analysis may not hold. Marketing compliance is therefore essential to maintaining the non-gambling classification. See section 5 below.

#### 3.2 Limb (b): Chance to Win

Section 4(1)(b) requires that participants "have a chance to win money or money's worth."

This limb is considered in the context of the overall activity. The relevant question is whether the opportunity to win is open to all participants (as in a conventional lottery or raffle) or whether it is contingent on something the participant must do or achieve.

**Application to REAP:**

Not all subscribers have a "chance to win" in the relevant sense. A subscriber who is eliminated on Day 1 has no chance to win draws on Days 7 through 30. Their elimination is not random — it is the direct consequence of their failure to meet the daily Zone 2 requirement. The "chance to win" is therefore not available to all paying participants; it is available only to those who have earned and maintained eligibility through their own physical performance.

This is materially different from a lottery (where all ticket-holders have an equal chance) or a raffle (where all entrants have an equal chance). REAP's draw pool is a meritocratic subset of subscribers, not the full subscriber base.

Accordingly, limb (b) is not satisfied in the conventional sense applicable to gambling.

**Counterargument to address:** The DIA or a court might observe that Active Survivors do have a "chance to win" in the sense that they may win or not win depending on the random selection. We acknowledge this. Our response is that the relevant "chance" in s4(1)(b) must be read alongside limb (c) — the holistic question is whether the activity is one where the dominant or defining feature is chance. In REAP, the dominant feature is skill-based qualification. The random draw is a selection mechanism among qualified participants, not the mechanism that determines who qualifies.

#### 3.3 Limb (c): Result Determined Wholly or Partly by Chance

Section 4(1)(c) requires that the result be "determined wholly or partly by chance."

This is the limb on which most promotional competition analysis focuses. The courts and the DIA have consistently held that "partly by chance" must be read as meaning that chance is a material or non-trivial determinant of the outcome. An activity where a skill-based qualification process is the primary determinant, and random selection is a secondary mechanism for selecting among an already-qualified pool, does not satisfy this limb in the manner intended by the Act.

**Application to REAP:**

The result of REAP — whether any individual participant wins a prize — is determined primarily by skill: 21 consecutive minutes of Zone 2 cardio, every day, without fail. To be eligible for the Day 30 draw, a participant must have completed 30 consecutive days of qualifying exercise. This is not a trivial qualification. It requires sustained, measurable physical effort. The random draw from the eligible pool is the final selection step, but only a participant who has performed at the required level for the required period can be in that pool.

By analogy: a competition that requires competitors to complete a marathon in under four hours, with the winner selected by random draw among all qualifiers, would not be a "game of chance." The qualification threshold is the dominant determinant. The random selection is merely a tiebreaker or distributor among those who have all earned eligibility.

REAP operates on the same structural principle.

### 4. Lottery-Specific Analysis

Section 4(2) defines "lottery" within the Act. A lottery is a scheme in which:
- Participants acquire a ticket, chance, or interest;
- In a draw or distribution in which the result is determined by chance;
- For money or money's worth.

REAP does not issue "tickets" or "chances." Eligibility in the draw pool is not acquired by payment — it is earned through performance. A subscriber who pays $13 but fails to meet the Zone 2 target daily does not "have a ticket" in any draw; they are ineligible and cannot participate. The structure is therefore not a lottery.

Furthermore, the definition of "lottery" in New Zealand law has historically been applied to schemes where consideration is paid in exchange for a probabilistic interest in a prize fund — the "pure lottery" model. REAP's subscription fee purchases a fitness service. The probabilistic element (whether a given Active Survivor is selected) is downstream of and subordinate to the skill-based qualification process.

### 5. Conditions for Maintaining Non-Gambling Classification

The analysis above is sound on the current REAP design. However, the non-gambling classification is conditional on the following features being maintained throughout the product's lifecycle:

| Condition | Rationale |
|---|---|
| The subscription fee must not be inflated beyond market rate for a fitness platform | Inflated fee may indicate disguised stake |
| The prize draws must remain incidental to the fitness product in all marketing materials | Emphasising prize over fitness undermines "incidental" characterisation |
| The prize pool must remain proportionate to subscription revenue (incidental, not dominant) | Dominant prize pool changes the nature of the product |
| Draw eligibility must remain contingent on active survival (skill-based daily compliance) | If all subscribers enter the draw regardless of compliance, the skill argument collapses |
| No additional or separate fee may be charged for draw eligibility | Separate entry fee would directly satisfy limb (a) |
| Zone 2 verification must remain technically robust and resistant to manipulation | If the skill-based elimination is shown to be easily gamed, the "skill" argument weakens |

### 6. Risk Assessment

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIA classifies REAP as a lottery | Low-Medium | High | This enquiry; strong legal arguments; redesign options available |
| DIA classifies REAP as Class 1 gambling (exempt) | Low | Medium | Class 1 exempt gambling requires minimal compliance; product can continue |
| Future regulatory change expands gambling definition | Low | Medium | Monitor DIA policy developments; build relationship with compliance group |
| Aggressive marketing inadvertently undermines classification | Medium | High | Marketing guidelines; legal sign-off on all prize-related copy |
| Wearable data manipulation allows users to game Zone 2 requirement | Low-Medium | High | Technical anti-cheat measures; wearable verification protocols |

### 7. Conclusion

On the current REAP design and the statutory provisions of the Gambling Act 2003, it is my opinion that the REAP prize draw structure is not gambling, not a lottery, and not a game of chance as those terms are defined in s4 of the Act. REAP is a promotional competition incidental to a commercial fitness subscription service. The prize draws serve as motivational incentives within a skill-based fitness game, and draw eligibility is earned through sustained physical performance — not purchased or randomly allocated.

The informal enquiry to the DIA has been filed to obtain written confirmation of this classification. Pending that response, Sport Waikato should proceed with product development on the basis of this analysis, with the compliance conditions in section 5 above strictly maintained.

I will report to the Board and Senior Leadership on the DIA's written response when received.
