## Overview

**Survive the Reap (REAP)** is a 30-day movement challenge and survival game launched by Sport Waikato in November 2026. This brief is designed to help healthcare professionals respond to patient questions about REAP, its medical safety protocols, and welfare considerations.

### Key Facts
- **Format:** 30-day survival competition via smartphone app
- **Movement requirement:** 21+ continuous minutes in Zone 2 heart rate intensity (50-70% max HR) daily
- **Cost:** NZ$13/month subscription
- **Participants:** Fitness-focused adults (18+)
- **Launch:** November 1, 2026
- **Prize pools:** $666 spot prizes across 7 draws throughout the season

---

## Movement Requirement Explained

### Zone 2 Heart Rate Definition
Zone 2 represents sustainable aerobic activity at **50-70% of maximum heart rate**. This is typically:
- Conversational pace (can speak but not sing)
- Equivalent to brisk walking or light jogging
- Heart rate for average 35-year-old: ~95-155 bpm

### Measurement Methods
Participants measure movement via:
1. **Connected wearables** (Apple Watch, Garmin, Fitbit, Polar, Whoop, Samsung Watch, Strava)
2. **Smartphone heart rate monitoring** (via TERRA API integration)
3. **Manual submission** for non-electronic devices (with Sport Waikato review)

### Medical Considerations
- **Low-intensity activity** — Zone 2 is sustainable, not exhaustive
- **Cessation mechanism** — Medical holds (7-day pause) available without elimination
- **Health screening optional** — No mandatory medical clearance, but encouraged for pre-existing conditions
- **Self-selection bias** — Participants self-select into a daily movement challenge; Health professionals should note that participants who struggle with the daily requirement can voluntarily exit with pro-rata refund

## Medical Welfare Protocols

### Incident Reporting Process
If a patient reports an incident related to REAP:
1. **Role of GP:** Document incident details, assess medical severity
2. **Patient action:** Patient should report via in-app "Welfare Incident" form or direct contact with Sport Waikato
3. **Sport Waikato response:** Welfare team logs incident, flags for escalation if serious
4. **Escalation path:** CEO review → Board notification (if welfare risk identified)

### Welfare Incident Categories
- **Tier 1 (Operational):** Minor injuries, temporary loss of motivation, device sync issues
- **Tier 2 (Medical):** Acute injury, cardiac event, mental health crisis → triggers board notification

### Medical Hold Process
If a participant is injured or unwell:
- **Duration:** Up to 7 days without elimination
- **Declaration:** Participant requests via in-app form
- **Approval:** Admin review within 24 hours
- **Continuation:** After hold expires, participant resumes daily 21-minute requirement or is eliminated

## Key Reassurance Points for Patients

### Safety Measures
1. **No maximum intensity** — Participants can exercise at their own pace above Zone 2
2. **Flexible timing** — 21 minutes can be split (e.g., 10 min + 11 min) but must be continuous within any single session
3. **Medical exemptions** — Participants can request up to 7-day hold for illness/injury without elimination
4. **Voluntary exit** — No penalty for discontinuation; pro-rata refund issued within 5 business days
5. **Welfare escalation** — Any incident is flagged to Sport Waikato leadership; no participant is silently dealing with issues

### Appropriate for Patients With:
- ✅ Pre-existing fitness habits
- ✅ Managed chronic conditions (hypertension, Type 2 diabetes, asthma) — with GP clearance
- ✅ Recent cardiac events (post-clearance from cardiologist)
- ✅ Joint conditions (can participate in low-impact Zone 2 activity)

### **Caution Advised** for Patients With:
- ⚠️ Uncontrolled or undiagnosed hypertension
- ⚠️ Recent acute coronary syndrome (< 6 months)
- ⚠️ Uncontrolled arrhythmia
- ⚠️ Severe heart failure (NYHA Class III-IV)
- ⚠️ Uncontrolled diabetes (blood glucose > 15 mmol/L)
- ⚠️ Acute or severe mental health crisis

## Questions & Answers for Healthcare Providers

### Q: Is REAP a gambling or betting product?
**A:** No. REAP is a promotional competition structured under the Gambling Act 2003 safe harbour provisions for lawful promotional competitions. Participants pay for a monthly subscription; the spot prizes are incidental incentives. It is not a lottery or betting product.

### Q: Who has approved REAP medically?
**A:** REAP has been reviewed by Sport Waikato's leadership for welfare protocols. Participants are not required to obtain medical clearance before entry but are encouraged to consult their GP for pre-existing conditions.

### Q: What are the elimination rules?
**A:** A participant is eliminated if they fail to achieve 21+ continuous minutes in Zone 2 on any single day. Elimination occurs at midnight NZT. Participants can declare a "Redemption Day" (up to 1 per season) to rest safely without elimination.

### Q: Can my patient take a break without being eliminated?
**A:** Yes. Participants can request a medical hold (up to 7 days) via the app without elimination. The hold must be approved by Sport Waikato admin (approval typically within 24 hours).

### Q: What if my patient is on medication that affects heart rate (e.g., beta-blockers)?
**A:** Zone 2 should be recalculated based on the participant's current resting and max heart rate. REAP's algorithm allows manual adjustment of heart rate zones. Participants are advised to consult their GP or sports medicine physician for accurate zone calculation if on heart rate-affecting medications.

### Q: Is there a medical advisor on the Sport Waikato REAP team?
**A:** Sport Waikato engages a medical advisor (typically a GP or sports medicine physician) as a consulting role for protocol review and incident escalation guidance. Direct medical inquiries should be directed to the participant's own healthcare provider.

### Q: What is the refund policy?
**A:** Participants receive a pro-rata refund if they exit voluntarily (within 48 hours of request) or are eliminated. Refunds are processed within 5 business days via original payment method (Stripe).

### Q: Who do I contact if I have concerns about a patient's participation?
**A:** Patient concerns can be escalated to Sport Waikato via info@sportWaikato.nz or through the in-app Welfare Incident reporting system. For urgent medical concerns, participants should contact emergency services (111) or their GP.

## Recommendations for Healthcare Providers

1. **Encourage self-awareness** — If patients have pre-existing conditions, recommend they discuss participation with their healthcare provider
2. **Monitor wearables** — Note that modern wearable tracking is generally reliable but should be verified against patient symptoms (e.g., if app shows Zone 2 but patient feels extreme fatigue, investigate further)
3. **Support medical holds** — If a patient is injured/unwell during REAP, encourage them to use the in-app medical hold rather than pushing through
4. **Escalate serious incidents** — If you become aware of a serious health incident related to REAP, encourage patient to report via the Welfare Incident system and consider contacting Sport Waikato medical advisor
