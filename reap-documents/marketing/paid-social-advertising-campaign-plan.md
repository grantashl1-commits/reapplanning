# Paid Social Advertising Campaign Plan

**Prepared by:** Marketing Team
**Date:** 2026-04-09
**Status:** Draft

## Purpose
Develop a premium paid social acquisition strategy for REAP that drives high-quality early access sign-ups, builds launch momentum, and positions the product as New Zealand’s most credible behaviour-change movement challenge.

## Campaign strategy
- Build a campaign funnel that moves audiences from awareness to registration with differentiated messages for discovery, consideration, and conversion.
- Use paid social to amplify Sport Waikato credibility, launch event news, and the October soft launch cohort.
- Create a data-driven acquisition engine that supports both local Waikato demand and national employer/referral pathways.
- Prioritise high-value creative and measurement so every dollar is tied to conversion performance and long-term brand equity.

## Channel mix
- Meta (Facebook + Instagram): broad reach, story-driven creative, local and interest-based targeting.
- TikTok: high-impact short-form video and creator-led storytelling for emotional participation moments.
- YouTube: premium video placements for launch films, how-to explainers, and soft launch testimonials.
- LinkedIn: targeted employer and workplace wellbeing audiences for team-based cohort recruitment.
- Display / native programmatic: awareness support for partner audiences and launch-related content.

## Creative direction
- Premiere-level launch films that show the product narrative, the living lab mission, and the emotional impact of surviving REAP.
- Authentic participant storytelling with real people, local faces, and credible movement experts.
- Bold brand creative that conveys urgency, community, and the simple “21 minutes a day” promise.
- Clear calls to action that emphasise early access, cohort registration, employer team sign-up, and community launch invites.
- Avoid gimmicky nostalgia. The visual tone should be contemporary, confident, and purpose-driven.

## Audience segments
### Core acquisition audiences
- Waikato adults aged 25–55 who are seriously interested in sustainable movement, wellbeing, or habit change.
- Local sports, fitness and walking community members with high engagement in regional groups.
- New Zealand adults with employer wellness or workplace wellbeing interests.
- Health-conscious urban audiences with a track record of participating in paid wellbeing programmes.

### Strategic audiences
- Employers, HR leaders, and workplace wellbeing managers for team and cohort registration.
- Local community group leaders, active transport advocates, and lifestyle coaches.
- Partner audiences from Sport Waikato funders, research collaborators, and health providers.

### Retargeting audiences
- Website visitors who viewed the launch registration page.
- Video viewers and ad engagers who watched 50%+ of launch content.
- Event attendees, soft launch cohort prospects, and early interest form submitters.
- Existing Sport Waikato community and newsletter audiences.

## Campaign structure
### Awareness
- Launch film ads to generate national and regional visibility.
- Story ads that highlight the problem REAP solves and the credibility of Sport Waikato.
- Employer-led ads for workplace cohorts, using LinkedIn and Instagram.

### Consideration
- Social proof ads with early cohort testimonials, partner endorsements, and “why REAP works” messaging.
- Educational creative that explains the 21-minute daily requirement, Redemption Days, and the prize draw model.
- Event-driven creative that promotes the Hamilton launch activation and media coverage.

### Conversion
- Lead generation ads and landing page traffic ads optimised for registration intent.
- Time-sensitive offers for first-wave access, cohort enrolment, and employer group sign-ups.
- Retargeted ads with direct CTA and clear benefits for joining before the first season.

## Creative formats
- 15–30 second hero launch videos for Meta, TikTok and YouTube.
- Short testimonial reels and proof-point snippets for feed and story placements.
- Carousel ads that walk users through the REAP journey in three steps.
- Collection ads with interactive, mobile-friendly registration experiences.
- LinkedIn sponsored content and message ads for employer and HR audiences.
- Dynamic remarketing creatives for users who visited the launch page.

## Measurement and optimisation
- Primary KPI: cost per confirmed early access registration.
- Secondary KPIs: landing page conversion rate, audience reach, view-through rate, and share of voice for launch-related content.
- Test creative variants continuously: hero film, testimonial, direct-response, and employer-focused content.
- Use campaign data to refine audiences, bids, and placements weekly.
- Build a dashboard with registration volume, campaign spend, CPA by segment, and partner attribution.

## Launch-specific tactics
- Run a “Hamilton first” burst that supports local launch event awareness and early cohort recruitment.
- Use a soft launch audience pool of influencers, ambassadors, and media attendees in retargeting.
- Offer exclusive early access or cohort entry for paid social responders.
- Layer in employer messaging as a premium offer: “Book your workplace REAP team trial.”

## Budget posture
- Assume a premium acquisition budget and optimise for quality rather than the lowest click cost.
- Allocate meaningful spend to creative production, testing, and ongoing campaign refinement.
- Reserve a portion of budget for retargeting high-intent audiences and employer-driven placements.

## Post-launch growth
- Extend paid social into sustained recruitment after launch, using retention stories and season progress content.
- Recycle premium launch assets for seasonal evergreen campaigns.
- Use campaign learnings to support Year 2 national scale and RST referral programmes.

## Success criteria
- Strong early access registration pipeline with measurable CPA.
- High-quality creative assets produced for continued launch support.
- Clear audience segmentation and a proven paid funnel from discovery to conversion.
- Paid social performance that validates national expansion assumptions and employer cohort potential.
