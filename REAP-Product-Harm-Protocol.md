# REAP — Product Harm and Welfare Protocol
**Classification:** Internal Operations | Sport Waikato Living Lab | April 2026
**Status:** Draft — requires board adoption before launch

---

## Purpose

This document exists because Sport Waikato has accepted a responsibility: there will be participants who are eliminated during periods of vulnerability. The probability is not zero. This document is our prepared response for when it happens.

The absence of a protocol is itself an ethical and legal liability. Having one — even an imperfect one — demonstrates that the risk was considered, documented, and managed with appropriate care. This document is that demonstration.

---

## Part 1: Categories of Harm

### 1.1 Psychological Harm

A participant receives an elimination notification during or immediately before a mental health crisis. The notification — arriving at 23:59 — is experienced not as a game outcome but as a confirmation of failure.

**Indicators we may become aware of:**
- Participant contacts Sport Waikato in distress following elimination
- Participant's content or behaviour on the platform indicates distress
- A third party (family member, friend) contacts Sport Waikato about a participant
- A participant makes statements about self-harm in any channel

**Severity levels:**
- Level 1: Participant expresses frustration or upset — does not indicate crisis
- Level 2: Participant expresses significant distress — language suggests real emotional impact
- Level 3: Participant expresses crisis-level distress — language includes self-harm, hopelessness, or indicates an emergency

### 1.2 Physical Harm

A participant injures themselves pursuing the 21-minute daily requirement (e.g., exercising while ill, exercising through pain to maintain their streak, or exercising in unsafe conditions at midnight).

**Indicators:**
- Participant reports injury or illness caused by participation
- Participant was hospitalised and contacts Sport Waikato about the event

### 1.3 Financial Harm

A participant argues they have been incorrectly eliminated and demands a refund, or a pattern of technical failures produces incorrect eliminations at scale.

**Note:** Financial harm and welfare harm may co-occur. A participant who was incorrectly eliminated and lost their fee during a period of illness has both a financial and a welfare claim.

## Part 2: Immediate Response Procedures

### Level 3 — Crisis Response (Priority: Immediate)

**Trigger:** Any communication from a participant that includes language indicating self-harm, a mental health emergency, or expressions of hopelessness following elimination.

**Response within 15 minutes:**
1. Acknowledge the message. Use warm, non-dismissive language: *"I can hear that you're going through a really hard time right now. I'm so glad you reached out."*
2. Signpost crisis resources immediately: *"Please contact Lifeline right now on 0800 543 354, or text or call 1737. These are free, 24/7, and they're there for exactly this."*
3. Do not leave the conversation. Stay present if the participant is still communicating.
4. Escalate immediately to the CEO (Leanne) — phone call, not message.
5. Document the communication (screenshot/log) — do not delete.
6. If there is any indication of immediate risk to life: contact NZ Police (111) with participant's contact details.
7. Do not offer a refund or reinstatement in this communication — that is a separate process and must not be the focus when someone is in crisis.

**After the immediate response:**
- CEO reviews the incident and determines whether a welfare check follow-up is warranted
- Incident logged in the welfare incident register
- Board notified (same day if serious)
- Legal advice engaged if any risk of liability

---

### Level 2 — Significant Distress (Priority: Same business day)

**Trigger:** A participant contacts Sport Waikato expressing significant emotional distress following elimination — distress that goes beyond ordinary disappointment.

**Response:**
1. Acknowledge and validate: *"Being eliminated is genuinely hard, especially if it happened at a difficult time. We hear you."*
2. Signpost support: *"If things are tough more broadly, the Mental Health Foundation's line is always available — 1737 or mentalhealth.org.nz. No obligation, just worth knowing it's there."*
3. Consider whether a goodwill gesture is appropriate (e.g., priority registration for Season 2, a courtesy acknowledgement). Not a financial refund. Not reinstatement.
4. Document the communication.
5. Escalate to Living Lab Lead for awareness.

---

### Level 1 — Distress or Frustration (Priority: Within 24 hours)

**Trigger:** A participant expresses frustration, upset, or disappointment following elimination — but language does not indicate crisis.

**Response:**
1. Acknowledge: *"Elimination is the core of the game and it does sting — that's by design, because the commitment matters. We're sorry it hit at a tough moment."*
2. If appropriate: signpost Season 2 registration.
3. If a legitimate dispute exists (device failure, technical error): follow the disputes process.
4. No crisis resources required unless the conversation escalates.

## Part 3: Physical Harm Response

**Trigger:** A participant reports injury, illness, or hospitalisation connected to REAP participation.

**Response:**
1. Express genuine concern. Do not be defensive. Do not immediately refer to Terms of Participation.
2. Ask what happened and listen.
3. Escalate immediately to CEO.
4. Document everything.
5. Engage legal advice within 24 hours.
6. Do not make any admission of liability without legal advice.
7. Consider whether the circumstances constitute a Fair Trading Act issue (e.g., if the product was promoted in a way that encouraged unsafe behaviour).

**Note:** The Terms of Participation and pre-participation health disclaimer do not eliminate Sport Waikato's liability if the product was designed or marketed in a way that foreseeably contributed to the harm. Legal advice is essential.

## Part 4: Incorrect Elimination — Process

**Trigger:** A participant contacts Sport Waikato claiming they were incorrectly eliminated (device failure, sync error, technical fault).

**Response — standard disputes process:**
1. Acknowledge within 24 hours: *"We've received your dispute and we're reviewing it. We'll respond within 3 working days."*
2. Review the evidence: check the participant's activity records in Supabase, their device sync log, and the elimination timestamp.
3. Determine: was the elimination technically correct (activity data showed <21 minutes), or was there a system error?
4. **If correct elimination:** Respond clearly and compassionately. Explain the data. Offer Season 2 priority registration. No reinstatement, no refund (per Terms).
5. **If system error:** Reinstate the participant immediately. Investigate the cause. Determine whether the error affected other participants. If a systematic error: board notification, public acknowledgement, and case-by-case resolution.

**Documentation:** All disputes are logged with the participant ID, date, claim, evidence reviewed, decision, and outcome.

## Part 5: Documentation Requirements

A welfare and disputes register must be maintained throughout each season. Minimum entries:

| Field | Required |
|-------|---------|
| Date and time of incident | Yes |
| Participant identifier (anonymised in reports) | Yes |
| Nature of contact (welfare / dispute / physical harm) | Yes |
| Severity level (1, 2, 3) | Yes |
| Response taken | Yes |
| Outcome | Yes |
| Escalated to CEO? | Yes/No |
| Board notified? | Yes/No |
| Legal advice sought? | Yes/No |
| Follow-up actions | Yes |

The register is reviewed at the end of each season and included in the end-of-season board report (anonymised).

## Part 6: Staff Training

Any staff member who may receive participant communications must be briefed on:
- The Level 1/2/3 response framework
- The crisis resources (Lifeline, 1737) — these must be accessible immediately, not looked up
- The escalation path (Living Lab Lead → CEO)
- The documentation requirement

A briefing session should be conducted before each season opens. The welfare protocol is not an emergency document — it is standard operating procedure.

## Part 7: Mental Health Partnership

Sport Waikato is pursuing a formal partnership with a NZ mental health organisation (candidate: Lifeline NZ, Mental Health Foundation of NZ, or a PHO mental health service) to:
- Co-develop the welfare protocol elements
- Review and approve the elimination notification copy
- Provide warm referral connections in elimination communications
- Co-research the wellbeing impacts of REAP participation across a season

This partnership is identified as a strategic priority. It turns the largest reputational risk into a genuine product differentiator and demonstrates that Sport Waikato took the mental health question seriously from Day 1.

**Action:** CEO to approach candidate organisations before Season 1 launch (June–July 2026 target).

## Part 8: Legal Framework

The welfare protocol operates within the following legal context:

- **Consumer Guarantees Act 1993:** Sport Waikato is not liable for elimination as a game outcome. It may be liable if the service was not delivered as described (e.g., incorrect technical elimination).
- **Fair Trading Act 1986:** Sport Waikato must not make claims about the product's health benefits that are false or misleading. The product is a game, not a clinical intervention.
- **Health and Safety at Work Act 2015:** Sport Waikato has a duty of care to its staff who manage welfare incidents. Staff handling Level 3 situations must have support available.
- **Privacy Act 2020:** All welfare incident records are personal information and must be handled accordingly — retained securely, not disclosed externally, available to the Privacy Commissioner on request.
